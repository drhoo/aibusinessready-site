AI Business Opportunity Assessment

Business: Harbour View Hotel (fictional)
Location: Newcastle, Australia
Industry: Small independent accommodation provider
Assessment: Illustrative example, October 2026

This is a sample, not a real client assessment. All business details, volumes and outcomes below are invented to demonstrate the report format.

Executive summary

The hotel may benefit from using AI to assist with routine enquiries, improve guest information and make its public information more consistent. We recommend a supervised email-drafting trial, not an autonomous booking chatbot.

Opportunity 1 — Routine enquiries

Problem: Assume 80 routine enquiries per week, requiring four minutes each.

Recommendation: Prepare approved answers to common questions, then test AI-generated draft replies that staff check before sending.

Potential benefit: A 50% reduction in staff time would release approximately 2.7 hours weekly, or 139 hours annually. This is an illustrative scenario, not a forecast or promised saving.

Safeguards: AI must not invent prices, disclose guest information, promise availability or confirm bookings without an authorised process.

Opportunity 2 — Guest information

Problem: Repeated enquiries may mean that important information is hard to find.

Recommendation: Create and maintain one reliable source covering facilities, parking, policies, check-in and frequent questions. Use that information consistently across the website and staff replies.

How to measure: Count routine enquiries before and after the change and check whether guest complaints increase or decrease.

Opportunity 3 — Online discoverability

Problem: Customers may research accommodation through search engines and conversational AI.

Recommendation: Check the clarity and consistency of public information about rooms, location, policies, facilities and contact options.

Limitation: No business can be guaranteed AI recommendations, placement, visibility or rankings.

Recommended first step

Run a two-week, human-approved email-drafting pilot.

  1. Document the ten most common guest questions.
  2. Build a set of approved answers.
  3. Test with fictional or non-sensitive examples.
  4. Have staff review every reply before sending.
  5. Compare time spent and error rates with the old process.

Success criteria

  • Less average staff time per enquiry
  • No rise in inaccurate replies or complaints
  • No unauthorised disclosure of customer information
  • Positive feedback from the staff using the process
  • Benefits justify subscription, setup and review costs

Costs and conclusion

Current tool pricing, privacy terms and compatibility with existing hotel systems would be verified for a real client. Implementation and subscriptions are not included in the assessment fee.

Conclusion: Start small. Verify results. Expand only if the trial delivers a measurable improvement without reducing service quality.
